Meet the team

How to play

Your job: keep 3 crew members healthy for 3 shifts, and run experiments to earn stars.

How a mission goes

  1. 1. Hear the Sun reportAt the start, Captain Rae shows you a real photo of the Sun. A ringed bright patch has fired before, so it may fire again. Each shift she tells you the storm chance.
  2. 2. Place your batteriesBatteries are your power. Your solar panels make 5 each shift. A shadow on the panels takes 1 away, and bright sun adds 1. The four rooms want more than you have, so drag each battery into a room and choose which room goes short.
  3. 3. Watch the shiftIt plays by itself. If the Sun flashes, a storm is on its way. You get 7 seconds to move 1 battery into the shelter as a last-chance save.

What each room does

  1. Air room: needs 2 batteriesIt keeps the air fresh. With only 1 battery the air gets thin and 1 crew member gets hurt. With none, the whole crew gets hurt.
  2. Farm: power it every shiftThe crew eats 3 meals a shift, 1 each. Each day starts with 6 fresh meals from Earth, so food runs out in shift 3. Lettuce needs 4 growth steps, and each battery gives 1 step a shift. So 2 batteries means a harvest in 2 shifts. A harvest makes 6 meals. With no power the lettuce wilts.
  3. Shelter: 1, 2 or 3 batteriesIt blocks storms: 1 for a small one, 2 for medium, 3 for big. Each battery it is short hurts one crew member.
  4. Lab: 1 experiment per batteryExperiments are why the crew came to the Moon, and each one adds to your score. But the lab does not keep anyone safe.

Your crew

  1. Nova, plant scientistRuns the farm. If she is hurt, the lettuce grows slowly.
  2. Kai, power engineerKeeps the solar panels working. If he is hurt, you get 1 fewer battery.
  3. Juno, medicHelps hurt crew heal. If she is hurt, the others cannot get better.

Crew go from fine, to hurt, to sick. A shift with fresh air and enough meals makes a hurt crew member one step better.

Stars

1 star for finishing. 2 stars for 3 experiments. 3 stars for 5 experiments and nobody hurt at the end.

My base

About the data

  • Real: each flare's date, time, size class, sunspot number and place on the Sun, and the clues built from them. Source: NASA DONKI (Space Weather Database Of Notifications, Knowledge, Information) at CCMC.
  • The daily mission uses yesterday's date (UTC). The game first asks NASA for the newest records. If it cannot reach NASA, it uses its saved copy from 14 Nov 2026, and says so. Practice missions pick a saved past day.
  • Simplified: in the game every flare brings a storm to the base. In real life only some flares send harmful particles toward the Moon. A calm day means DONKI lists no flare for that date.
  • Real science, squeezed: a flare's light reaches the Moon in about 8 minutes and the harmful particles come later. The game squeezes that wait into a few seconds.
  • Batteries: the base makes 5 a shift, loses 1 when a shadow falls on the solar panels, and gains 1 in bright sun. That is a game rule. It is based on a real idea, that near the Moon's poles the Sun stays low so hills cast long shadows, which still needs checking against NASA.
  • Game rules, not data: the storm chance meter, battery counts, meals, experiments, crop growth, upgrades, and the crew rules. They are small whole numbers so a child can count them: 3 crew eat 1 meal each per shift, and 1 lab battery runs 1 experiment. The true part of the crew rules is that a sick crew cannot do all its work.
  • The Sun picture is a real photo from NASA's Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO) for the mission date, fetched through the Helioviewer service. It is taken in ultraviolet light, which our eyes cannot see, so NASA colours it red. Bright patches are the busy regions where flares happen. If the photo cannot load, the game shows a drawing instead. The ring that marks a sunspot is placed from the NASA flare record, or estimated from the Sun's rotation on a day the spot did not fire, so it is approximate.
  • Shifts are 8 hours each on the crew's 24 hour clock (UTC). The crew's facts are sample lines that still need checking against NASA.
  • Voices: the Voice button makes the voices talk at 2x. A Skip button moves on by one sentence. The game uses your device's built-in voice to read lines aloud. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. The voice sounds different on each device, and you can switch it off with the Sound button.
  • Your base is saved in this browser only. There is no account.
  • Daily totals: when you finish the daily mission, the game sends 3 things to a shared counter: the mission date, your stars, and whether your whole crew stayed healthy. It sends no name and nothing about you. The game shows only the totals for the day.
